VISUALIZATION 1 - Booking Rate by Star Rating (Bar Chart)
Answers: Q1
----------------------------------------------------------------------

What to show:
  Bar chart where the x-axis is hotel star rating (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) and the
  y-axis is the booking rate (percentage of impressions that resulted in a
  booking) for each star category.

Key numbers to use (already computed from the full dataset):
  0 stars  - 1.65% booking rate  (337,794 impressions)
  1 star   - 1.27% booking rate  (34,514 impressions)
  2 stars  - 2.17% booking rate  (1,788,994 impressions)
  3 stars  - 2.80% booking rate  (3,897,419 impressions)
  4 stars  - 3.34% booking rate  (3,045,848 impressions)
  5 stars  - 2.58% booking rate  (812,961 impressions)

What the chart should look like:
  Color the bars consistently (one color, not a rainbow). Label the y-axis as
  "Booking Rate (%)" and x-axis as "Hotel Star Rating". Add a data label or
  annotation on each bar showing the exact percentage. Title: "Booking Rate
  by Hotel Star Rating".

Insight to discuss in the write-up:
  Booking rate rises with star rating from 1 through 4 stars, but 5-star
  hotels actually convert lower than 4-star hotels (2.58% vs 3.34%). This is
  counterintuitive and interesting - it likely reflects that 5-star hotels are
  premium-priced, so users browse them but are less likely to commit. The sweet
  spot for conversion is 4-star. This sets up Q2: star rating alone does not
  explain booking behavior without also accounting for price.

Important note for the write-up:
  These rates are computed across all sessions including algorithmically ranked
  ones. Higher-rated hotels also tend to appear in better positions (position
  bias), so the relationship is partly driven by display rank, not just quality.
  This is why we need Q2.

VISUALIZATION 2 - Booking Rate vs. Display Position (Line Chart)
Answers: Q1 and Q2 setup
----------------------------------------------------------------------

What to show:
  Line chart (or bar chart) where x-axis is display position (1 through ~20)
  and y-axis is booking rate. This is the position bias visualization and is
  the most striking chart in the entire dataset.

Key numbers to use (top 10 positions):
  Position 1  - 14.14% booking rate
  Position 2  -  9.47% booking rate
  Position 3  -  7.17% booking rate
  Position 4  -  5.76% booking rate
  Position 6  -  4.50% booking rate
  Position 7  -  3.74% booking rate
  Position 8  -  3.21% booking rate
  Position 9  -  2.73% booking rate
  Position 10 -  2.56% booking rate
  Position 15 -  1.60% booking rate

  (Note: positions 5 and 11 have anomalously low counts - skip or annotate
  them as data artifacts rather than plotting them as normal data points.)

What the chart should look like:
  A smooth downward curve, sharply dropping from position 1 to 4, then more
  gradually. Use a line chart with markers. Label the axes clearly. Title:
  "Booking Rate by Display Position". Consider adding a note or annotation
  pointing to position 1 labeling it "14.1% - 9x higher than position 15".

Insight to discuss in the write-up:
  The drop from position 1 (14.1%) to position 2 (9.5%) alone is larger than
  the entire booking rate at position 8. Users overwhelmingly click and book
  hotels shown at the top of results regardless of quality. This is position
  bias - the central methodological challenge in this dataset. It means any
  analysis of what drives bookings must control for position, otherwise we
  are just measuring Expedia's algorithm choices, not actual user preferences.
  This directly motivates using random_bool=1 sessions as unbiased ground truth
  in Q2.

This is probably the most important visualization in the report. It is visually
dramatic and immediately motivates the methodological approach.

VISUALIZATION 3 - Heatmap: Star Rating x Price Tier -> Booking Rate
Answers: Q1
----------------------------------------------------------------------

What to show:
  A 2D heatmap where rows are star rating categories (0-5) and columns are
  price quartiles (cheap / mid-low / mid-high / expensive), and each cell
  shows the booking rate for hotels in that combination. Color intensity
  represents booking rate - darker = higher conversion.

How to create the price tiers:
  Bin price_usd into 4 quartiles from the data:
    Cheap:      $0 - ~$80
    Mid-Low:    ~$80 - ~$122 (around median)
    Mid-High:   ~$122 - ~$200
    Expensive:  $200+
  (Use DuckDB to query booking rate grouped by ntile(4) of price_usd and
  prop_starrating - ask Francisco or run analysis/data_report.py first)

What the chart should look like:
  A 6x4 grid of colored cells. Use a sequential color scale (white to dark
  blue, or white to dark red). Label every cell with its booking rate
  percentage. Title: "Booking Rate by Star Rating and Price Tier".
  Add axis labels: y-axis "Star Rating", x-axis "Price Tier".

Insight to discuss in the write-up:
  This chart reveals which star-price combinations actually convert best. We
  expect 4-star hotels in the mid-range price tier to be the sweet spot. Very
  cheap hotels (even with decent star ratings) may have lower conversion because
  users are skeptical of quality. Very expensive hotels convert poorly despite
  high stars because of price sensitivity. The heatmap shows the interaction
  between these two variables simultaneously - something neither a pure star
  chart nor a pure price chart can reveal on its own.

VISUALIZATION 4 - Expedia Price Competitiveness vs. Booking Rate (Bar Chart)
Answers: Q1
----------------------------------------------------------------------

What to show:
  A bar chart comparing booking rates when Expedia is cheaper than competitors
  versus when Expedia is more expensive. Use the comp_rate columns (aggregated
  across competitors 1-8 - take the most commonly available one or the majority
  vote across available competitors).

  Three categories on x-axis:
    Expedia cheaper (-1): booking rate when Expedia beats competitors on price
    Same price (0):       booking rate when Expedia matches competitors
    Expedia pricier (+1): booking rate when Expedia is more expensive

What the chart should look like:
  Three bars, clearly colored (e.g. green for cheaper, gray for same, red for
  pricier). Title: "Booking Rate by Expedia Price Competitiveness vs. Competitors".
  Note in the caption that competitor data is only available for ~10-45% of
  impressions depending on the competitor, so these results reflect a partial
  subset of the data.

Insight to discuss in the write-up:
  Users are more likely to book when Expedia is price-competitive. This is the
  expected result but the magnitude matters - a large gap would suggest price
  shopping is a dominant behavior, while a small gap would suggest users are
  relatively price-insensitive (perhaps because they are already on Expedia
  and trust it). The high missingness in competitor data is itself interesting:
  it means most hotels in most sessions had no competitor pricing information
  captured, which limits how much weight we can put on this analysis.

TOOL RECOMMENDATION
==============================================

For PM1, Tableau is recommended for all four of these charts. The data is too
large to load fully into Tableau directly - export aggregated results from
DuckDB to a small CSV first, then import that into Tableau.

Steps:
  1. Run the relevant query in Python using DuckDB (see analysis/data_report.py
     for examples of the DuckDB pattern)
  2. Export the aggregated result to a small CSV (e.g. 6 rows for the star
     rating chart)
  3. Import that CSV into Tableau and build the chart

For the heatmap (Visualization 3), Tableau handles this well natively with
its built-in heatmap / highlight table chart type.

All charts must have:
  - A descriptive title
  - Labeled axes with units
  - A caption or annotation in the write-up explaining what insight it gives
    into which research question
  - Clean formatting - no chart junk, no 3D bars, no rainbow color schemes
