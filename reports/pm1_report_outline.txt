HEADING: Research Questions

--- Q1 ---
Question: What hotel attributes and competitive pricing factors are most
associated with click and booking rates on Expedia?

Paragraph outline:
  - State the question clearly in one sentence.
  - Explain who cares: Expedia's product and ranking team, and hotel revenue
    managers who set prices and decide whether to offer promotions. Expedia
    processes millions of searches daily - even a small improvement in
    understanding what drives conversion has significant revenue impact.
  - Note the challenge: since Expedia's algorithm already ranks hotels by
    predicted conversion, observed booking rates conflate hotel quality with
    display position. This is addressed by including position as a control
    variable and by using the random_bool natural experiment (explained in
    Section 1) as a check on our findings.

--- Q2 ---
Question: Beyond where a hotel is displayed, which hotel and search
attributes most strongly predict whether a user will book - and are those
effects real or just artifacts of Expedia's ranking algorithm?

Paragraph outline:
  - State the question clearly in one sentence.
  - Explain why it goes beyond Q1: Q1 is descriptive (what correlates with
    bookings). Q2 asks whether those correlations hold up once you remove the
    confounding effect of display position. This matters because Expedia's
    algorithm creates a circular problem: it shows better hotels at the top,
    so top hotels get more bookings, which makes them look better.
  - Explain the natural experiment as the key to answering this: the 29.6%
    of sessions where results were randomly sorted (random_bool=1) give us
    unbiased ground truth. In those sessions, booking behavior reflects real
    user preferences, not algorithmic ranking. We can compare results across
    both groups to isolate the true effect of hotel attributes.
  - Who cares: any stakeholder who wants to know whether improving a hotel's
    star rating, review score, or pricing genuinely increases bookings - or
    whether display rank is the only thing that matters.

Paragraph outline (2-3 sentences):
  - Describe what the chart shows: a bar chart of booking rate (y-axis, %)
    by hotel star rating (x-axis, 0-5 stars) across all 9.9 million
    impressions in the dataset.
  - State the main insight: booking rate increases from 1-star (1.27%) to

    not bookers, suggesting price sensitivity limits conversion at the top end.
  - Connect to Q1: star rating alone is an incomplete predictor of conversion.
    The heatmap in Visualization 3 shows why: price tier interacts heavily
    with star rating, and 5-star cheap hotels are actually the top-converting

  - Optional methodological note: these rates include algorithm-ranked sessions,
    so higher-rated hotels may also appear in better positions. The effect of
    star rating independent of position is examined in Q2.

Paragraph outline (3-4 sentences):
  - Describe what the chart shows: a line chart of booking rate (y-axis)
    by display position 1-20 (x-axis), with TWO lines. The dark line
    (random_bool=0) represents sessions where Expedia's algorithm ranked
    results. The light line (random_bool=1) represents sessions where
    hotels were displayed in random order.
  - State the most striking insight: in algorithm-ranked sessions, the hotel
    at position 1 is booked at nearly 20% - roughly 10x higher than position
    15. The drop from position 1 to position 2 alone (~6 percentage points)
    is larger than the entire booking rate at most lower positions. This is
    position bias: users overwhelmingly book from the top of the list.
  - Explain what the second line adds: in randomly sorted sessions
    (random_bool=1), the same steep drop disappears. Booking rates start
    around 1.9% at position 1 and stay roughly flat across positions. This
    is the natural experiment in action - when rank is random, rank stops
    predicting bookings. The entire dramatic effect in the dark line is
    driven by Expedia's algorithm placing its best candidates first.
  - Connect to Q2: this chart is the primary motivation for our second
    question. If position explains almost everything, do hotel attributes
    matter at all once you strip out display rank? The random_bool sessions
    give us the cleanest setting to find out.
  - NOTE FOR CHART: the legend currently shows "0" and "1" - before
    submitting, update the legend labels to "Algorithm-ranked (random_bool=0)"
    and "Randomly sorted (random_bool=1)" so readers understand the lines
    without needing to know the column name. Also note the anomalous dips
    at positions 5 and 11 - these are data artifacts (very few impressions
    at those positions) and can be mentioned briefly in the write-up or
    annotated on the chart.
  - If including the click rate chart as a supporting figure: note that the
    click rate chart (click_bool) shows the exact same two-line pattern,
    confirming that position bias affects both the decision to click and the
    decision to book. Click rates start at ~21% at position 1 in algorithm
    sessions vs ~14% in random sessions.

Paragraph outline (3-4 sentences):
  - Describe what the chart shows: a 6x4 heatmap where each cell shows the
    booking rate for hotels at a given star rating (rows: 0-5 stars) and
    price tier (columns: Cheapest / Mid-Low / Mid-High / Expensive). Darker
    blue = higher booking rate, ranging from 0.558% to 5.246%.
  - State the key finding: the most booked segment is 5-star hotels in the
    cheapest price tier (5.25% booking rate), followed closely by 5-star
    mid-low (4.87%) and 4-star cheapest (4.99%). Across every star rating
    tier, cheaper hotels convert better - the Expensive column is the lightest
    (lowest conversion) across all rows. This reveals a clear price
    sensitivity effect that the simple star rating bar chart (Vis 1) could
    not show.
  - State the secondary finding: the pattern also reveals that price matters
    more than stars for conversion. A 3-star cheap hotel (3.55%) outperforms
    a 5-star expensive hotel (1.97%). Users are not simply buying the best
    hotel - they are buying the best value. This has direct implications for
    hotel pricing strategy.
  - Connect back to Q1 and forward to Q2: this chart answers Q1 by showing
    that price tier is a key driver of conversion, interacting with star
    rating in a non-trivial way. For Q2, this motivates including both
    price_usd and prop_starrating as predictors in our logistic regression,
    along with their interaction.
